# Model card: BridgeWatch risk models

## Models
- Three scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` models with the same settings (`src/bridgewatch/model.py`): up to 1,000
  trees, learning rate 0.06, 63 leaves, L2 1.0, early stopping on a 10% validation split.
  - **Q1:** P(a bridge that is not Poor becomes Poor within h years), h = 2, 4, 8.
  - **Q3:** P(an open bridge is posted for load, restricted or closed within h years), h = 2, 4.
  - **Q4:** P(a "miss" within 4 years: becomes Poor, posted/closed, or any component rating falls 2+ points).
- Monotone constraint: predicted risk cannot fall as deck, superstructure, substructure, culvert or lowest rating gets worse.
- Categorical codes (state, owner, material, design, scour, load-rating method, …) are learned from training rows; codes seen
  fewer than 50 times become missing.

## Features (bridge's own record and earlier years only)
Current ratings and lowest rating; changes over 2 and 4 years; years at the current lowest rating; rating drops and inferred
repairs in the past 8 years; age; years since reconstruction; traffic, truck share, detour length; deck area, span, spans, skew,
lanes; operating and inventory load rating; inspection interval; posting status; scour code; material, design, deck type and
protection; owner; functional class; NHS; state; latitude and longitude. A test checks that features in year t do not change when
later years are deleted (`tests/test_features.py`).

## Intended use
- Ranking bridges for a closer look, budgeting and inspection-policy review by agencies, researchers and the public.
- Not for decisions about a specific bridge's safety, load posting or inspection interval; those belong to the owner agency.

## Training and evaluation data
- FHWA National Bridge Inventory, every year 1992–2025, record type 1, IDs linked across renumberings (`src/bridgewatch/linking.py`).
- Time split: a fold with test year T trains on base years T−h−7 … T−h (outcomes known by T) and tests on all eligible bridges in T.
- Hold-out states: Iowa, Texas, Pennsylvania, California and Georgia are excluded from training for every method and scored separately.
- Labels are censored, not guessed: if a bridge leaves the inventory before the horizon without the event, it is dropped.

## Known limitations
- Inspector-to-inspector variation in ratings is label noise the model cannot remove.
- Bridges renumbered without a matching description or location keep two separate histories (about 1.1% of IDs a year disappear).
- Posting changes that follow a load-rating re-analysis, not deterioration, are counted as Q3 events.
- Calibration was checked on the backtest folds; probabilities for 2026+ assume the 2014–2021 relationships still hold.
- The 8-year Q1 model does not beat time-in-condition in the 2013 hold-out states (top 1%: 47% vs 52%).
- Individual predictions are less stable than the aggregate results: retraining on the same data moved one Iowa bridge from
  77% to 85% P(Poor within 4 years), because multithreaded tree building is not bit-for-bit reproducible. Rankings and backtest
  metrics are stable; treat a single bridge's percentage as approximate.
